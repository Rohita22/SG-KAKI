# Kopi Rush content research

The game models the shared Singapore kopitiam ordering grammar, not fixed measured ratios. Individual stalls vary in strength and proportions.

Core rules represented in the recipe data:

- Default `kopi` / `teh`: condensed milk.
- `O`: no milk; sugar remains unless `kosong` is also said.
- `C`: evaporated milk, normally with sugar.
- `kosong`: no sugar.
- `siew dai`: less sweet; `gah dai`: more sweet.
- `gao`: stronger/thicker; `po`: weaker/thinner.
- `peng`: iced; `tarik`: pulled until frothy.
- Yuan yang combines coffee and tea.
- Milo Dinosaur is iced Milo topped with extra Milo powder.

Following product-direction feedback, kopi, teh, and Milo are treated as ready-made bases. Players only select a base and add condensed milk, evaporated milk, sugar, ice cubes, or Milo powder. Kopi and teh share the O, C, kosong, and peng grammar; Milo can be served with or without condensed milk, and Milo Dinosaur adds ice plus extra Milo powder on top.

Portions are deliberately game units rather than fixed tablespoons: standard sweet drinks use two portions, `siew dai` uses one, `gah dai` uses three, and `kosong` uses none. This teaches relative order-code meaning while acknowledging that real stall ratios vary.
